# 05 — Field Operations Manual

## 1. Mission preparation

Before departure:
- charge phone, camera, audio recorder and power bank;
- check available storage on each device;
- confirm date/time and camera settings;
- prepare consent explanation in the relevant language;
- download required maps, model files and reference material;
- test the app in airplane mode;
- plan a second copy of important recordings.

## 2. Documentary capture protocol

For each scene or interview, capture:
1. Context note: where, when and what is happening.
2. Wide establishing image/video, when appropriate.
3. Detail shots of tools, materials and process.
4. Interview audio/video with consent.
5. Names, pronunciation and preferred attribution.
6. Local vocabulary and explanations.
7. Follow-up questions and uncertainties.

Do not interrupt a living cultural practice solely to obtain footage. Follow the contributor's direction and community protocols.

## 3. Insta360 X6 workflow

1. Inspect lens guards/lenses and clean with a suitable cloth.
2. Check battery, storage and date/time.
3. Record a short test clip.
4. Confirm exposure and audio.
5. Capture the original 360 footage.
6. Preserve original files and camera metadata.
7. Transfer copies to the phone or workstation when practical.
8. Reframe in Insta360 Studio.
9. Export derivatives for horizontal and vertical edits.
10. Link exports to the original source record.

The exact camera menu names and supported recording modes depend on firmware. Use the current camera manual for mode-specific settings.

## 4. Samsung S25 Ultra companion capture

Use the phone for:
- still photographs and contextual video;
- interview notes;
- voice memos;
- GPS metadata where appropriate;
- quick review and backup.

Keep exposure, white balance and frame rate consistent within a sequence where possible. Avoid relying on digital zoom for critical documentary details.

## 5. Interview method

Start with broad, non-leading questions:
- “Can you show me how this is made?”
- “What do you call this tool or step?”
- “Who taught you this technique?”
- “How has the process changed?”
- “Is there anything you would prefer not to record or publish?”

Ask permission before close-ups of faces, private spaces, sacred objects or restricted practices.

## 6. Metadata checklist

For each asset:
- [ ] Unique asset ID.
- [ ] Capture date/time and timezone.
- [ ] Creator/operator.
- [ ] Location only when safe and permitted.
- [ ] Subject/description.
- [ ] Language.
- [ ] Consent scope.
- [ ] Attribution preference.
- [ ] Restrictions and embargoes.
- [ ] Link to original source.

## 7. Daily ingest and backup

At the end of the day:
1. Stop recording and safely close camera files.
2. Copy original media to a primary storage location.
3. Make a second copy on a separate device when possible.
4. Verify sample files open and file counts match.
5. Enter field notes and asset links.
6. Record any missing files or uncertain metadata.
7. Keep restricted material clearly marked.
8. Do not erase camera cards until backups are verified.

## 8. Responsible documentary practice

- Obtain informed consent.
- Explain intended audiences and distribution.
- Offer anonymity when practical.
- Respect requests to stop or exclude material.
- Do not publish private or culturally restricted knowledge without permission.
- Confirm translations and names with knowledgeable speakers.
- Provide a correction/contact path where possible.
- Avoid presenting AI-generated interpretations as the community's own words.

## 9. End-of-mission archive

Create a mission folder containing:
- original media;
- edited derivatives;
- field notes;
- transcripts;
- consent records;
- source references;
- AI-generated drafts;
- final outputs;
- a README describing folder contents and restrictions.
